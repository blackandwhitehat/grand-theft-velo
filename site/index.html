curl -fsSL https://grandtheftvelo.com/install.sh | bash

The one command. Paste it into a terminal on the Pi.

No gaming PC. No mod. No soldering. No capture card. Nothing plugged into the console and nothing changed on it. No account to create and nothing sent to us. Do not like it? Press Stop ride and your trainer and your PS5 are exactly as they were.

You do not need the steering block or a brake to start. A trainer and the controller you already have are enough for a first ride: your legs do the throttle, the controller steers (by thumb, or strapped to the handlebars so the bars steer), and the bike brakes by itself when you stop pedaling.

The real thing

This is the whole setup

No lab, no rack of gear. A bike on a trainer in front of the TV, and a Raspberry Pi hidden behind it.

How it works

A Pi at your trainer plays the controller

A Raspberry Pi sits next to your indoor trainer. It reads your power over Bluetooth and plays the part of the controller through PS5 Remote Play. The PS5 stays plugged into the TV, so you ride watching the normal picture. Nothing is modified on the console and nothing is plugged into it.

Pedal

Your watts become the throttle, from a light squeeze to flat out. Sprint and it mashes the sprint button for you.

Steer

A steering block under your front wheel reads the handlebar angle. No block? Strap a controller to the bars.

Play

Pair your controller to the Pi from the same page. Every button, both sticks and the touchpad pass straight through, so the whole game is still in your hands.

Everything is set up and tuned from a page on your phone.

Why this one is different

No mod. The real console. No video lag.

The bike projects that came before, built for GTA 5, all worked the same way: a mod loaded into the PC version of the game. That needs a gaming PC, a modded copy of the game, and a mod that keeps up with every update. It cannot work on a console at all.

No mod

The game is untouched, on a stock PS5. To the console, the Pi is just a Remote Play controller.

Native picture

You watch the PS5 on your TV as normal, at its native resolution and frame rate. The Remote Play video only goes to the Pi; you never look at it.

No video lag

The picture comes straight from the console to the TV, so there is no streaming delay or compression in what you see. Only your inputs travel over the network.

Survives update day

There is no mod to fall out of step with a game patch. When the game updates, you just ride.

Progress

Where it is today

This is an early project that works on its author's bike. It has been ridden on exactly one setup: a PS5, a Raspberry Pi 4, an original Tacx Neo, an Elite Sterzo Smart and a DualSense. Read this before you buy anything.

Working well

  • One command installs everything on a Pi
  • Setup and tuning from a web page
  • Pedaling power drives the throttle in the game
  • Handlebar steering with an Elite Sterzo
  • Your controller pairs to the Pi and stays fully usable

In testing

  • Ride log with heart rate, as a file for Strava
  • Braking by stopping pedaling, no brake switch needed
  • Steering with a controller on the bars, and a second in your hands
  • Other trainers (FTMS), trainer resistance and a brake switch
  • Pairing and waking the PS5 from the web page

Coming soon

  • Automatic Strava upload
  • Your route on the game's map
  • Tracked ride and pedal to play modes
  • Hills you can feel

Have a different trainer? Try it and open an issue with what happened. That is the most useful thing anyone can do for this project right now.

Current goals

Your ride, on the map

Riding the game is the start. The goal is that a ride through the game counts like a ride outside: your route drawn on the game's own map, real numbers, and all of it on Strava when you stop.

Ride the game Working

Power, steering and gamepad become one PS5 controller.

Ride file and heart rate In testing

Every ride saved with time, power, cadence and heart rate, in a file Strava, Garmin Connect and intervals.icu accept. Automatic upload comes next.

Route tracking Next

The Pi already receives the game's video. It will read your heading from the compass, your distance from the game's own readouts, and your position from the full map, so the route is the true shape of the streets you rode.

Two ways to ride Planned

Tracked ride and pedal to play. See below.

Hills Planned

The trainer gets harder when the road in the game climbs. The game shows no elevation on the ground, so this is the hardest part.

Tracked ride

Logged with its route on the map, and only while your character is on a bicycle. Steal a car and the log pauses until you are back on a bike. What lands on Strava is honest: every mile was pedaled.

Pedal to play

No map, effort only. Do anything in the game with the controller, on foot or in a car. The catch: you have to pedal the whole time. Stop, and the game pauses on you. The whole game becomes the workout.

Get going

What you need

Required

  • A PS5 on your home network, with Remote Play turned on
  • A Raspberry Pi 4 or 5, ideally on a network cable
  • A Bluetooth trainer, power meter or cadence sensor

Optional

  • Elite Sterzo Smart for steering, or strap your controller to the bars
  • A controller paired to the Pi, for every other button
  • A heart rate strap
  • A brake switch on a GPIO pin
curl -fsSL https://grandtheftvelo.com/install.sh | bash

One line, about 10 to 15 minutes on a Pi 4. Then open the page it prints on your phone and follow the steps. The full guide, questions and troubleshooting are in the README.
